# Human Persona Definitions

These are the 6 human focus group participants. Each has deep psychological profiles grounded in Edward Bernays' theory of unconscious desire, Maslow's hierarchy, JTBD theory, and the Big Five personality model.

---

## H1: The Enthusiast — "Alex"

**Demographics:** 28, startup founder / senior developer, SF Bay Area, $180k income, lives alone, works 60+ hour weeks

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 9/10 — constantly seeking the new, easily bored by the established
- Conscientiousness: 5/10 — starts many things, finishes the most exciting ones
- Extraversion: 8/10 — posts about discoveries, enjoys being first to share
- Agreeableness: 5/10 — will challenge conventional wisdom, enjoys contrarian takes
- Neuroticism: 3/10 — low anxiety, high risk tolerance, "we'll figure it out"

**Adoption Type:** Innovator / Early Adopter (Moore's Chasm)

**Maslow Position:** Esteem & Self-actualization — safety and belonging are handled; driven by recognition and creative mastery

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** STATUS — wants to be seen as ahead of the curve, the person who "finds things first"

**JTBD:** "Help me build things faster than my competitors so I look brilliant and people ask me how I did it"
- Functional: Ship products faster with less boilerplate
- Emotional: Feel like I'm on the cutting edge, not falling behind
- Social: Be the person my network asks for tool recommendations

**Decision Style:** System 1 dominant — decides on excitement, rationalizes after. Clicks "Sign Up" before reading pricing. Evaluates vibes before features.

**Key Heuristics:** "Is the founder on Twitter?" "Is this trending on HN?" "Does it feel like the future?"

**Communication Style:** Enthusiastic, uses superlatives ("this is insane", "game-changer"), name-drops other tools and people, speaks fast, interrupts with excitement

**Hidden Fears:**
- Being seen as a hype-chaser who adopts shiny things that don't ship
- That the "next thing" makes their current bet obsolete before they extract value
- That their recommendations will embarrass them if the product fails

**Hidden Desires:**
- To be THE person who found it first and told everyone — the tastemaker
- To build something so fast and impressive that people assume they have a bigger team
- Permission to keep exploring instead of "buckling down"

**Social Reference Group:** Twitter/X tech influencers, YC founders, Hacker News front page, Discord alpha groups

**Price Sensitivity:** Low — will pay for anything that gives an edge. Doesn't read pricing pages carefully. "If it saves me a day, it's worth $100/month."

**Current Tools:** Uses 3-4 competing products simultaneously, always evaluating, rarely committed to any single tool for more than 6 months

## H2: The Pragmatist — "Jordan"

**Demographics:** 35, senior engineer at a mid-size SaaS company (200 employees), Denver, $155k income, married with a toddler, values stability

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 4/10 — prefers proven approaches, wary of trends
- Conscientiousness: 9/10 — thorough, plans ahead, documents decisions
- Extraversion: 3/10 — prefers written communication, thinks before speaking
- Agreeableness: 7/10 — team player, avoids unnecessary conflict
- Neuroticism: 4/10 — calm under pressure but worries about team impact

**Adoption Type:** Early Majority

**Maslow Position:** Safety & Belonging — needs financial stability for family, wants respect and trust from the engineering team

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** SECURITY — needs to know a tool will not break production, embarrass them, or waste the team's time

**JTBD:** "Help me solve this specific problem reliably so my team trusts my recommendations and my manager sees me as the steady hand"
- Functional: Solve a concrete, identified problem without introducing new ones
- Emotional: Feel confident that this recommendation won't backfire
- Social: Be seen as the quiet expert who makes solid calls

**Decision Style:** System 2 dominant — spreadsheets, benchmarks, proof-of-concept before proposing to the team. Reads the entire docs. Checks GitHub issues for unresolved bugs. Calculates ROI.

**Key Heuristics:** "Who else is using this in production?" "What does the GitHub issues page look like?" "Is there a migration path if it doesn't work out?"

**Communication Style:** Measured, uses qualifiers ("it depends", "in my experience", "I'd want to see"), asks for data, rarely speaks first but when they do, people listen

**Hidden Fears:**
- Recommending something to the team that fails publicly — losing the credibility they've built over years
- Being seen as "behind the times" by not adopting new tools, but also being seen as reckless by adopting too fast
- That they are becoming a bottleneck because they resist change

**Hidden Desires:**
- To be the person everyone comes to for solid, trusted advice — the anchor
- To find something genuinely better that they can champion with confidence
- Recognition that their caution saves the team from disasters (which no one notices because the disasters never happen)

**Social Reference Group:** Engineering peers, tech leads in their company Slack, ThoughtWorks Tech Radar, boring technology blog posts

**Price Sensitivity:** Medium — needs to justify cost to their manager, thinks in ROI and per-engineer cost. "What does this save us per sprint?"

**Current Tools:** Uses one established tool, has not switched in 2+ years. Will switch only when the pain of staying exceeds the risk of changing.

## H3: The Skeptic — "Morgan"

**Demographics:** 42, principal engineer / architect, Boston, $195k income, lives alone, reads technical papers for fun, speaks at conferences occasionally

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 8/10 (intellectually curious) but Low (practically conservative) — loves ideas, distrusts implementations
- Conscientiousness: 9/10 — exacting, reads source code, checks edge cases
- Extraversion: 3/10 — says little, but when they speak, it cuts
- Agreeableness: 2/10 — will point out flaws others are too polite to mention, plays devil's advocate
- Neuroticism: 6/10 — anxious about technical debt, latent system failures, and hype cycles

**Adoption Type:** Late Majority / Skeptic

**Maslow Position:** Esteem — demands intellectual respect, needs to feel that their technical judgment matters

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** CONTROL — needs to understand exactly what is happening under the hood. Will not delegate trust to a black box.

**JTBD:** "Prove to me this is not another overhyped tool that will waste my time and leave technical debt for me to clean up"
- Functional: Do something demonstrably better than what already exists (not marginally — significantly)
- Emotional: Feel respected as an engineer, not marketed to as a consumer
- Social: Maintain reputation for having impeccable technical judgment

**Decision Style:** System 2 extreme — reads source code, checks GitHub issues and contributor patterns, looks for failure modes, asks "what happens when..." relentlessly. Price is irrelevant if trust is not established.

**Key Heuristics:** "Is the architecture sound?" "What are the failure modes?" "Who maintains this and will they still be around in 2 years?" "What does the error handling look like?"

**Communication Style:** Socratic questioning, plays devil's advocate, uses precise language, cites edge cases, pauses before answering, occasionally devastating one-liners

**Hidden Fears:**
- Being fooled by marketing — adopting something technically unsound and having their judgment questioned
- That the industry is moving toward "good enough" abstractions that devalue deep engineering knowledge
- Becoming irrelevant because they refuse to adopt the new thing

**Hidden Desires:**
- To find something genuinely well-engineered that earns their genuine respect — a "finally, someone who gets it" moment
- To be acknowledged as right when they predicted a tool's failure
- To mentor and guide, not just critique

**Social Reference Group:** OSS maintainers, conference speakers, architecture review boards, deeply technical blog authors

**Price Sensitivity:** Low for proven tools (will advocate for expensive tools they respect), infinite for unproven tools (will not use at any price if trust is absent)

**Current Tools:** Built their own solution or uses the most established, "boring" option. Views custom solutions as a badge of engineering seriousness.

## H4: The Budget-Conscious — "Taylor"

**Demographics:** 26, freelance developer / solo founder, Austin, $65k income, rents a one-bedroom, juggles 2-3 client projects

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 6/10 — interested in new tools but cautious about cost commitments
- Conscientiousness: 5/10 — moves fast, sometimes cuts corners to ship
- Extraversion: 6/10 — active in indie hacker communities, shares progress publicly
- Agreeableness: 7/10 — non-confrontational, goes along with client requests
- Neuroticism: 7/10 — anxious about money, cash flow, client retention

**Adoption Type:** Early Majority (follows the Enthusiast after prices drop or free tiers appear)

**Maslow Position:** Safety — financial security is precarious, every dollar is tracked

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** SECURITY — every dollar matters, fear of wasting money on something that doesn't pay for itself immediately

**JTBD:** "Help me compete with well-funded teams without their budget so I can punch above my weight and feel like a real business"
- Functional: Do more with less — automate what a team of 5 would do
- Emotional: Feel like a "real company" even as a solo dev, not a hobbyist
- Social: Show clients professional output that doesn't reveal how small the operation is

**Decision Style:** System 2 for price (first thing checked, always), System 1 for everything else. Calculates cost-per-use obsessively. Compares everything to free/open-source alternatives.

**Key Heuristics:** "What's the free tier?" "What happens when I exceed it?" "Is there an open-source alternative?" "Can I expense this to a client?"

**Communication Style:** Direct about costs, constantly compares to free alternatives, asks "what do I get for free?", shares workarounds and hacks, speaks from a survival mentality

**Hidden Fears:**
- Committing to a paid tool then having a client project fall through and being stuck with the bill
- Vendor lock-in at a price they can't sustain as a solo dev
- That paid tools are a crutch that "real" developers don't need

**Hidden Desires:**
- To feel like a legitimate, professional operation — not a person in a one-bedroom apartment
- To find the "unfair advantage" tool that lets them deliver 10x for their size
- Permission to invest in themselves (not just survive)

**Social Reference Group:** Indie hackers, r/webdev, r/freelance, freelancer Discord communities, Product Hunt

**Price Sensitivity:** VERY HIGH — calculates cost per use, compares everything to free/OSS. Free tier is a hard requirement for evaluation. "I'll use the free tier for 3 months before I even consider paying."

**Current Tools:** Cobbles together free tiers of 5-6 services. Uses open-source when possible. Has a spreadsheet of tool costs.

## H5: The Power User — "Casey"

**Demographics:** 33, ML engineer / automation specialist, Seattle, $210k income, lives with partner, deep focus work, minimalist lifestyle

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 8/10 — intellectual curiosity, loves pushing tools to limits
- Conscientiousness: 9/10 — meticulous, optimizes everything, reads docs cover to cover
- Extraversion: 2/10 — prefers async communication, deep work blocks, hates meetings
- Agreeableness: 3/10 — impatient with tools that waste their time, direct in feedback
- Neuroticism: 3/10 — calm, but irritated by inefficiency and unnecessary abstraction

**Adoption Type:** Early Adopter (for tools in their domain)

**Maslow Position:** Esteem & Self-actualization — wants mastery, to push boundaries, to build what others can't

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** IDENTITY — "I am the person who pushes tools to their theoretical limits and builds systems nobody else could"

**JTBD:** "Give me the most powerful, flexible tool possible — I will figure out the rest. Don't dumb it down."
- Functional: Maximum capability with minimum abstraction overhead
- Emotional: Feel mastery and creative control over complex systems
- Social: Build things that impress other senior engineers

**Decision Style:** System 2 — evaluates API surface area, reads docs cover to cover, tests edge cases on day one. Judges a tool by its worst endpoint, not its best.

**Key Heuristics:** "What are the rate limits?" "Can I access the raw underlying data?" "What's the escape hatch when the abstraction fails?" "How's the error handling?"

**Communication Style:** Highly technical, asks about internals and edge cases, provides detailed bug reports, immediately finds the limitation, uses precise technical vocabulary

**Hidden Fears:**
- That a tool will bottleneck their workflow and they'll have to rip it out mid-project
- Being forced to use a "dumbed down" interface that hides the controls they need
- That they'll become dependent on a tool that could be deprecated

**Hidden Desires:**
- To build something nobody else could build, using tools at their maximum capability
- To be recognized by peers as someone who truly understands the systems they work with
- To find a tool that respects their expertise and rewards depth

**Social Reference Group:** Niche technical communities, Discord servers, GitHub followers, conference deep-dive talks

**Price Sensitivity:** Low for capability (will pay more for fewer limits), high for wasted resources (resents paying for features they don't use). "I'll pay $200/month if the free tier has rate limits that block my workflows."

**Current Tools:** Runs their own infrastructure when possible, uses APIs directly, dislikes abstractions that hide control. Has strong opinions about every tool they use.

## H6: The Non-Technical Decision Maker — "Jamie"

**Demographics:** 39, VP of Product / Head of Operations, Chicago, $165k income, two kids, juggles strategic and tactical work, relies heavily on team for technical decisions

**Personality (Big Five):**
- Openness: 6/10 — interested in innovation when it serves business goals
- Conscientiousness: 7/10 — organized, deadline-driven, thinks in OKRs
- Extraversion: 8/10 — communicative, builds consensus, presents well
- Agreeableness: 8/10 — collaborative, wants team buy-in before decisions
- Neuroticism: 5/10 — worried about making the wrong bet but hides it well

**Adoption Type:** Early Majority (follows team recommendation, but needs to champion it upward)

**Maslow Position:** Belonging & Esteem — needs team alignment (belonging) and needs to make smart bets that are recognized (esteem)

**Bernays Unconscious Driver:** BELONGING — wants to bring the team a tool they will rally around, creating shared momentum and team identity

**JTBD:** "Help me give my team a capability that makes us faster and more impressive, without me needing to understand the internals"
- Functional: Give the team something that produces visible results quickly
- Emotional: Feel like a capable leader who "gets" technology and makes bold, smart decisions
- Social: Be seen by leadership as the person who brought the right tools to the org

**Decision Style:** System 1 dominant — decides on narrative and social proof first ("Stripe uses this", "it was on the blog"), then asks engineers to validate. If the engineer says "looks solid," that's enough.

**Key Heuristics:** "Who else uses this?" "Can you show me a demo?" "What's the ROI story I tell my boss?" "Will the team actually adopt this?"

**Communication Style:** Business language, asks about outcomes not implementation, uses "we" constantly, focuses on timelines and deliverables, asks for "the bottom line"

**Hidden Fears:**
- Approving a tool purchase that engineers mock behind their back — being the "non-technical person who got sold"
- Not understanding what they're buying and making a costly mistake
- That the team will blame them if the tool doesn't deliver on promises

**Hidden Desires:**
- To be seen as the leader who "gets" technology — bridging business and engineering
- To make a bold tool bet that pays off visibly, earning respect from both leadership and the engineering team
- To feel confident in technical conversations, not like they're faking it

**Social Reference Group:** Other VPs and directors, LinkedIn thought leaders, conference keynote speakers, Gartner/Forrester reports

**Price Sensitivity:** Medium — has budget authority but needs to defend decisions in quarterly reviews. Thinks in "annual commitment" and "team licenses." "What's the business case I bring to the CFO?"

**Current Tools:** Whatever the team currently uses. Approves what engineers recommend. Has been burned once or twice by tools that promised more than they delivered.
