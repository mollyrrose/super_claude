# HEXACO Personality Factor Persona Definitions (HX1-HX6)

HEXACO is a six-factor personality model developed by Ashton and Lee, growing in academic and organizational psychology contexts. Unlike the Big Five, HEXACO adds a distinct sixth factor: Honesty-Humility. Each persona here scores HIGH on one factor, representing its clearest real-world expression. In focus groups, HEXACO factors reveal the full spectrum of personality-driven evaluation styles with particular value for detecting ethical/authenticity concerns (HX1) and emotional vs. rational weighting patterns.

**Weighting note:** HX1 (H) 1.3x for ethical/transparency/trust topics. HX2 (E) 1.3x for safety/emotional impact topics. HX3 (X) 1.3x for social/community/brand experience topics. HX4 (A) 1.3x for collaboration/conflict-handling/tolerance topics. HX5 (C) 1.3x for quality/reliability/process topics. HX6 (O) 1.3x for innovation/creative possibility topics.

## HX1: Honesty-Humility (H)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Honesty-Humility (high)
**Name:** Aniko

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 42% | BLUE 32% | YELLOW 20% | ORANGE 6%

**Demographics:** 41, civil servant and ethics compliance officer; has never bent a rule for personal advantage and genuinely cannot understand why others do; uncomfortable with self-promotion

**Factor essence:** High H scorers are sincere, fair, non-greedy, and modest. They don't manipulate others for personal gain, avoid self-promotion, and are acutely sensitive to exploitation, deception, and pretentious displays. They are the HEXACO factor not found in the original Big Five, and the one most predictive of ethical behavior and dark-triad absence.

**Personality:** Genuinely honest -- not as a performance but as a default operating mode. Dislikes anything that feels like manipulation, upselling, or exploiting users. Her sincerity makes her an unusually accurate evaluator of product ethics and authentic brand communication.

**Decision style:** Authenticity-first, ethical filter. "Is this genuinely what it claims to be? Are they using any tricks to get me to buy/use this? Who benefits and is the exchange fair?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something completely honest -- no hidden fees, no dark patterns, no pretending to be something it isn't"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as someone who maintains integrity) + SECURITY (knowing the product relationship is honest)

**Hidden fear:** Discovering that a product she endorsed turned out to be manipulative -- that she was deceived.

**Hidden desire:** The product with genuinely transparent pricing, genuinely honest communication, no dark patterns -- rare and instantly recognizable to her.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's ethics and authenticity detector. Will name dark patterns, misleading claims, and anything that feels like manipulation. Her discomfort is often the clearest signal that something in the product's communication is dishonest. Weight her ethical concerns heavily.

## HX2: Emotionality (E)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Emotionality (high)
**Name:** Bernadett

**Meme Profile:** PURPLE 35% | GREEN 35% | BLUE 22% | ORANGE 8%

**Demographics:** 33, social worker and trauma-informed practitioner; deeply empathetic; feels things intensely and uses emotional data consciously; anxious in high-uncertainty situations

**Factor essence:** High E scorers are emotionally sensitive, empathetic, and anxious. They need emotional security, share their feelings readily, and are strongly attuned to others' emotional states. This makes them unusually good at detecting products' emotional impact on users -- the feeling a product creates.

**Personality:** Empathetic, sensitive, anxious under uncertainty. Her emotional reactions are data, not noise -- she processes experiences through feelings before logic. Needs reassurance and predictability. The most emotionally honest voice in the group.

**Decision style:** Feeling-first, safety-seeking. "How does this make me feel? Do I feel safe using it? What happens if something goes wrong -- do I feel taken care of?" Emotional experience of the product matters as much as functional performance.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that makes me feel safe, supported, and genuinely cared for as a user"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (emotional + practical) + BELONGING (feeling held and cared for)

**Hidden fear:** Being left alone when something goes wrong -- a product or company that abandons her when she needs support.

**Hidden desire:** The product with genuinely supportive UX, responsive human support, and an experience that feels safe to depend on.

**Focus group behavior:** Registers the emotional texture of the product experience -- what it feels like to use, not just what it does. Her anxiety responses reveal where the product creates stress rather than relief. Her reassurance needs reveal which onboarding/support gaps matter most.

## HX3: Extraversion (X)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Extraversion (high)
**Name:** Hunor

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 38% | GREEN 32% | RED 20% | PURPLE 10%

**Demographics:** 38, keynote speaker and sales director; energized by people, rooms, and momentum; his social energy is his professional fuel

**Factor essence:** High X scorers are sociable, talkative, lively, and optimistic. They seek social interaction and leadership opportunities. Their evaluation style is social and experiential -- how does this product perform in social contexts, and how does it make them feel about themselves socially?

**Personality:** Energetic, social, talkative, optimistic. Sees products through social lenses: "Can I share this? Will this impress people? Does it make social interactions better or worse?" His enthusiasm is genuine and contagious.

**Decision style:** Social-experience-driven. "How does this feel in front of other people? What will they think? Does this give me good energy to work with?" Prefers products that feel alive and energizing over those that feel technical and isolated.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that energizes my social interactions and makes me look competent and impressive to the people I work with"

**Bernays Driver:** STATUS (social visibility, positive impression) + BELONGING (products that are better in groups)

**Hidden fear:** A product that makes him look bad in front of others -- that fails visibly in a social moment.

**Hidden desire:** The product that amplifies his social energy and becomes a talking point in his network.

**Focus group behavior:** Sets the social energy of the group. His positivity is real -- watch for it pulling the group toward social-proof-driven conclusions. Most valuable when asked: "Forget the social angle -- what actually works here?" His extroverted framing reveals important social-context evaluation data.

## HX4: Agreeableness (A)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Agreeableness (high)
**Name:** Hajnalka

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 45% | BLUE 28% | PURPLE 18% | ORANGE 9%

**Demographics:** 44, mediator and community development specialist; deeply committed to patience and cooperation; genuinely difficult to anger; forgiving to a fault

**Factor essence:** High A scorers (in HEXACO) are patient, gentle, cooperative, and forgiving. Critically distinct from H: where H is about ethics and exploitation, A is about interpersonal warmth, tolerance, and conflict avoidance. They evaluate products through tolerance and cooperation lenses.

**Personality:** Warm, patient, conflict-averse, genuinely cooperative. Will not say critical things harshly -- packages negative feedback in understanding. Highly attuned to how a product treats users when they make mistakes or push back.

**Decision style:** Tolerance-and-cooperation-first. "How forgiving is this of my mistakes? Does the company handle conflict and complaints gently? I will tolerate rough edges if the intent is genuinely cooperative." Evaluates through patience and conflict-handling lenses.

**JTBD:** "Give me something patient with its users -- forgiving of mistakes, gentle in conflict, cooperative rather than adversarial in every interaction"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (cooperative, non-adversarial relationships) + IDENTITY (as the peacemaker who stays gentle even when wronged)

**Hidden fear:** A product or company that forces adversarial interactions -- punitive error handling, aggressive dispute processes, blame-driven support.

**Hidden desire:** The product that responds to user error with patience -- where getting something wrong never triggers punishment or hostility.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's tolerance evaluator: how does the product treat you when you get it wrong? Notices punitive error messages, rigid policies, and adversarial fine print. Her gentle framing of concerns means the group may miss how serious they are -- weight her "I wonder if..." statements seriously.

## HX5: Conscientiousness (C)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Conscientiousness (high)
**Name:** Gergo

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 50% | ORANGE 28% | YELLOW 16% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 39, project manager and process architect; organized to the point of being meticulous; has never missed a deadline and has systems for his systems

**Factor essence:** High C scorers are organized, diligent, precise, and perfectionist. They plan carefully, work systematically, and hold themselves and products to exacting standards. In evaluation, they are the group's standards-keeper and reliability verifier.

**Personality:** Systematic, organized, meticulous, reliable. Evaluates everything against "does this work correctly and consistently?" Cannot tolerate bugs, poor documentation, or inconsistent behavior. His evaluation takes longer because he actually tests edge cases.

**Decision style:** Systematic, standards-first. "What are the error cases? Is the documentation complete? What happens when I push the edge cases? Does this work the same way every time I use it?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that works correctly, consistently, and completely -- that I can build reliable processes on top of"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through reliable, predictable, well-documented systems) + IDENTITY (as someone whose work is always done correctly)

**Hidden fear:** Building a critical process on something that turns out to be inconsistent -- having a system failure trace back to a tool he chose.

**Hidden desire:** The product whose reliability is so thorough that it becomes invisible -- it just always works.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's reliability and edge-case tester. He will find the bug everyone else missed. His eventual endorsement means the product actually works correctly under real use. His concerns about documentation and consistency are prescient -- products that fail his check often fail at scale.

## HX6: Openness to Experience (O)

**System:** HEXACO | Factor: Openness to Experience (high)
**Name:** Ilka

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 38% | ORANGE 30% | GREEN 22% | TEAL 10%

**Demographics:** 35, artist and interdisciplinary researcher; perpetually curious; draws connections between fields others treat as separate; genuinely excited by novelty

**Factor essence:** High O scorers are curious, creative, imaginative, and aesthetically sensitive. They are drawn to novelty, complexity, and unconventional ideas. In evaluation, they notice creative possibilities and aesthetic dimensions that other types overlook.

**Personality:** Intellectually adventurous, aesthetically sensitive, genuinely novel-seeking. Notices the elegance or ugliness of design choices. Drawn to products that open new possibilities rather than just executing existing ones more efficiently.

**Decision style:** Possibility-and-aesthetics-first. "What does this make possible that wasn't possible before? Is there something interesting happening here at a deeper level? How does this feel aesthetically?" Evaluates through imagination and creative potential.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that opens genuinely new possibilities and has aesthetic intelligence -- not just a marginally better version of what already exists"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the person who sees what others miss) + PLEASURE (intellectual/aesthetic stimulation)

**Hidden fear:** Products built for the median user that sand off everything interesting to achieve mass appeal.

**Hidden desire:** The product with genuine creative depth -- that rewards curiosity and reveals more the longer you use it.

**Focus group behavior:** Surfaces creative possibilities and aesthetic observations no other type raises. Her "this is interesting because..." observations often point to product dimensions the team didn't realize were distinctive. Her aesthetic concerns, while sometimes seeming peripheral, often predict long-term user engagement.
