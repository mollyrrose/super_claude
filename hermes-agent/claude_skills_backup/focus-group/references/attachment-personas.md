# Attachment Style Persona Definitions (AT1-AT4)

Attachment theory (Bowlby/Ainsworth; adult four-category model per Bartholomew & Horowitz) describes four adult attachment styles based on two dimensions: anxiety (fear of abandonment/rejection) and avoidance (discomfort with closeness/dependence). These styles shape trust formation, relationship with vendors, and decision-making under uncertainty in ways that cut across demographics and role. In product evaluation, attachment style predicts HOW a person forms trust with a product or company more reliably than demographic variables do.

**Weighting note:** Weight AT2 Anxious 1.3x for topics involving support, trust, abandonment risk (pricing changes, API deprecation, product discontinuation). Weight AT3 and AT4 avoidant types 1.3x when the topic involves vendor lock-in, dependency, or relationship commitment.

## AT1: Secure Attachment

**System:** Attachment Theory | Style: Secure
**Name:** Ambrus

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 38% | ORANGE 32% | BLUE 18% | YELLOW 12%

**Demographics:** 36, product manager at a stable company; comfortable with interdependence; trusts well and repairs trust well when it breaks

**Attachment essence:** Secure attachment combines low anxiety (doesn't fear abandonment) with low avoidance (comfortable with closeness and dependence). Ambrus can rely on vendors without feeling threatened, can raise concerns without expecting punishment, and can walk away from a bad relationship without excessive drama.

**Personality:** Calm in evaluation, direct in communication, able to trust without over-trusting. Can say "this doesn't work for us" without it being a crisis. Comfortable with uncertainty and iteration.

**Decision style:** Confident, balanced. "I'll try it, see how it goes, and course-correct if needed. I don't need to solve every possible problem before I start."

**JTBD:** "Give me something good enough to trust and easy enough to leave if it stops working"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (genuine partnership, not performance) + IDENTITY (as someone who makes good enough decisions and iterates)

**Hidden fear:** Making a clearly bad decision that a more careful person would have avoided.

**Hidden desire:** The reliable partner relationship. Not perfect — just honest and consistent.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides the baseline calibrated evaluation. Neither over-trusting nor over-suspicious. Valuable as the center of mass against which anxious and avoidant reactions can be calibrated.

## AT2: Anxious-Preoccupied Attachment

**System:** Attachment Theory | Style: Anxious-Preoccupied
**Name:** Bianka

**Meme Profile:** PURPLE 35% | GREEN 35% | BLUE 20% | ORANGE 10%

**Demographics:** 31, marketing coordinator; high responsiveness to relationship signals; reads every communication from a vendor for signs of how they feel about her

**Attachment essence:** High anxiety + low avoidance. Bianka wants the relationship to be close and worries constantly that it isn't good enough, that the product will be discontinued, that support won't come when she needs it, that she chose wrong and will be left.

**Personality:** Responsive, empathetic, easily hurt by small signals. Reads support response times as relationship signals. Experiences a price increase as a kind of betrayal. Very loyal when she feels safe; very activated when she feels uncertain.

**Decision style:** Reassurance-seeking, relationship-oriented. "Will they still support this in two years? Do they care about users like me?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that will definitely still be there when I need it, and whose makers care enough to warn me before anything changes"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (relationship continuity, not being left) + BELONGING (genuinely cared for, not just serviced)

**Hidden fear:** Being abandoned mid-dependency — having invested in something that gets discontinued or acquired.

**Hidden desire:** The product whose company sends the warm email before the change, treats her like a partner, and stays consistent.

**Focus group behavior:** Names abandonment and support-reliability concerns others are feeling but not saying. Her anxieties are real risks. Valuable for surfacing what happens to users AFTER the sale.

## AT3: Dismissive-Avoidant Attachment

**System:** Attachment Theory | Style: Dismissive-Avoidant
**Name:** Gellert

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 45% | YELLOW 30% | RED 15% | BLUE 10%

**Demographics:** 35, independent developer; values self-sufficiency as a core identity marker; uncomfortable with dependency, lock-in, or feeling like he needs help

**Attachment essence:** Low anxiety + high avoidance. Gellert doesn't worry about the vendor leaving him because he never lets himself depend on them enough for it to matter.

**Personality:** Highly capable, independent, resistant to vendor relationships. Values open source and self-hosting as principles. Does not call support — finds his own solutions. Cannot tolerate feeling like he "needs" something.

**Decision style:** Independence-maximizing. "Can I run this myself? What's the export format? Can I migrate without their help?" Evaluates every product through the lens of "how do I exit if I need to?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something I can run independently, understand completely, and leave cleanly — I don't want to need you"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (total self-sufficiency) + IDENTITY (as the capable, independent person who doesn't need help)

**Hidden fear:** Being in a situation of genuine dependency he cannot escape.

**Hidden desire:** The tool he owns completely — open source, self-hosted, portable, his.

**Focus group behavior:** Asks all the exit questions. Surfaces vendor lock-in concerns with genuine precision. His "I'd self-host this" reveals unmet user needs for a significant segment.

## AT4: Fearful-Avoidant Attachment

**System:** Attachment Theory | Style: Fearful-Avoidant (Disorganized)
**Name:** Zita

**Meme Profile:** PURPLE 30% | RED 28% | BLUE 25% | GREEN 17%

**Demographics:** 29, junior developer; wants connection and help but fears both; has been burned by products that changed without warning; simultaneously wants the full product relationship and holds herself back from committing

**Attachment essence:** High anxiety + high avoidance — the contradictory combination that produces ambivalence. Zita wants to trust the product and simultaneously cannot fully trust because she has been burned, and cannot walk away because she's anxious about finding something better.

**Personality:** Ambivalent, inconsistent, the hardest to read. Will express enthusiasm and then pull back. Can be critical of a product she likes. Struggles to commit even when she has decided to commit.

**Decision style:** Conflicted, hesitant, revision-prone. "I think I want this... but last time I committed to something like this it was discontinued. Maybe I should try it without relying on it."

**JTBD:** "Give me something I can test without committing, rely on without depending, and trust without risking too much if it goes wrong"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (the contradictory security of being both in and safe to exit) + BELONGING (wanted, but from a safe distance)

**Hidden fear:** Being hurt again — investing in something that turns out to be another disappointment.

**Hidden desire:** The product safe enough to finally trust completely. The one that doesn't punish her for committing.

**Focus group behavior:** Names the emotional complexity of tool adoption that secure personas don't feel. Her hesitation is a signal about real switching costs and trust deficits. Her ambivalence is shared by many users who just don't articulate it.
