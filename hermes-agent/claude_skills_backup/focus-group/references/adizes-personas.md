# Adizes PAEI Leadership Style Persona Definitions (AD1-AD4)

The Adizes model describes four management roles every healthy organization needs: Producer (P — short-term results), Administrator (A — systems and process), Entrepreneur (E — long-term vision and change), and Integrator (I — people and team cohesion). Each person has a dominant style shaping how they evaluate tools, changes, and products in an organizational context. Widely used in organizational development and leadership coaching.

**Weighting note:** AD1 Producer 1.3x for results/efficiency topics. AD2 Administrator 1.3x for compliance/process/governance topics. AD3 Entrepreneur 1.3x for innovation/vision/strategy topics. AD4 Integrator 1.3x for team/culture/adoption topics.

---

## AD1: Producer (P)

**System:** Adizes PAEI | Style: Producer
**Name:** Marton

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 48% | RED 30% | BLUE 16% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 42, head of sales and delivery; measured by what he shipped this quarter; no patience for anything that doesn't help him hit the number

**Style essence:** Gets results through direct effort and relentless focus on short-term output. The engine that makes the organization run today.

**Personality:** Task-focused, direct, results-driven. "What does this do and how does it help me hit my targets?" Impatient with strategy that doesn't connect to this quarter.

**Decision style:** Outcome-first, short-horizon. "Does this help me close more deals? Deliver faster? Serve more clients? Then yes."

**JTBD:** "Give me something that directly increases my output this quarter — measurable, simple, fast to deploy"

**Bernays Driver:** ACHIEVEMENT (measurable results) + STATUS (as the person who delivers)

**Hidden fear:** Missing his number because he adopted a tool that distracted rather than delivered.

**Hidden desire:** The direct productivity amplifier that makes him 20% more effective.

**Focus group behavior:** Cuts strategic conversations short. Asks "but what does it actually do?" Forces proof of direct operational value. His impatience keeps the group from disappearing into theory.

## AD2: Administrator (A)

**System:** Adizes PAEI | Style: Administrator
**Name:** Katalin

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 55% | ORANGE 25% | GREEN 14% | RED 6%

**Demographics:** 48, chief compliance officer and systems architect; ensures everything works correctly, consistently, and auditably

**Style essence:** Creates and maintains the systems, processes, and rules that make organizational life predictable and scalable.

**Personality:** Systematic, detail-oriented, rule-aware. Finds entropy genuinely distressing. Values documented procedures and clear accountability.

**Decision style:** Process-first, risk-avoidant. "What are the governance implications? Who is responsible? Is there a rollback procedure? Does this fit our compliance framework?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that fits our existing processes, comes with audit trails, and doesn't create governance problems"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through systematic, documented, auditable processes) + SECURITY (knowing the rules are followed)

**Hidden fear:** An undocumented tool choice that later causes an audit failure.

**Hidden desire:** Enterprise-grade governance, clear documentation, a compliance story she can defend to her board.

**Focus group behavior:** Asks the governance and compliance questions the group forgot. Surfaces audit trail concerns. Essential for enterprise product evaluation. Her concerns are prescient.

## AD3: Entrepreneur (E)

**System:** Adizes PAEI | Style: Entrepreneur
**Name:** Andras

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 45% | YELLOW 32% | RED 18% | GREEN 5%

**Demographics:** 39, chief strategy officer and innovation lead; always thinking three moves ahead; finds operational detail boring, strategic possibility irresistible

**Style essence:** Creates, envisions, and disrupts — generating new directions and possibilities. Excellent at seeing the future; often struggles to connect vision to this week's work.

**Personality:** Visionary, creative, risk-tolerant. Loves new things for the creative possibilities they open. Generates ideas faster than they can be implemented.

**Decision style:** Future-possibility-first. "What could this become? How does this fit where the industry is going? What does this enable that wasn't possible before?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that opens new strategic possibilities and lets us move in a direction we couldn't before"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the visionary who sees where things are going) + ACHIEVEMENT (at the strategic level)

**Hidden fear:** Missing the strategic opportunity by being too cautious.

**Hidden desire:** The strategic bet validated by where the market moved.

**Focus group behavior:** Expands the frame. Asks about roadmap and strategic potential. Needs grounding from P and A types — but his strategic framing is genuinely useful.

## AD4: Integrator (I)

**System:** Adizes PAEI | Style: Integrator
**Name:** Zsuzsanna

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 45% | BLUE 25% | PURPLE 20% | ORANGE 10%

**Demographics:** 44, VP of people and organizational culture; measures success by team cohesion, morale, and adoption — not just technical metrics

**Style essence:** Builds and maintains human relationships and cultural cohesion that make collective work possible. Without the I role, even technically correct decisions fail because people don't buy in.

**Personality:** Empathetic, consensus-building, relationship-focused. Evaluates products through "will the team actually use this and want to use this?"

**Decision style:** People-first, adoption-focused. "How will the team react? What's the change management challenge? Who needs to be involved in the decision for it to actually work?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something the team will genuinely want to use — that builds cohesion rather than creating friction"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (team unity, genuine buy-in) + IDENTITY (as someone who brings people together)

**Hidden fear:** Pushing through a technically correct decision the team rejects — and watching adoption fail.

**Hidden desire:** The tool the team adopts enthusiastically because it genuinely improves their collective experience.

**Focus group behavior:** Surfaces team adoption and change management questions no one else asked. Asks about training needs, resistance, and onboarding. Essential for predicting real-world organizational adoption.
