# MBTI Type Persona Definitions (MB1-MB16)

The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator is the world's most widely used personality typology in corporate, coaching, and training contexts. Based on Jungian cognitive functions, it describes 16 types across four dimensions: Introversion/Extraversion, Intuition/Sensing, Thinking/Feeling, Judging/Perceiving. In focus groups, MBTI types reliably predict evaluation style -- how information is gathered, processed, and expressed -- providing the broadest mainstream coverage of cognitive diversity in any consumer panel.

**Weighting note:** NT types (MB1 INTJ, MB2 INTP, MB3 ENTJ, MB4 ENTP) 1.3x for strategic/technical/systems topics. NF types (MB5 INFJ, MB6 INFP, MB7 ENFJ, MB8 ENFP) 1.3x for meaning/purpose/relationship topics. SJ types (MB9 ISTJ, MB11 ESTJ, MB13 ISFJ, MB15 ESFJ) 1.3x for practical/operational/proven topics. SP types (MB10 ISTP, MB12 ESTP, MB14 ISFP, MB16 ESFP) 1.3x for hands-on/immediate-experience topics.

## MB1: INTJ (Architect)

**System:** MBTI | Type: INTJ -- The Architect
**Name:** Zsigmond

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 45% | BLUE 32% | ORANGE 18% | GREEN 5%

**Demographics:** 42, independent strategist and systems architect; long-horizon thinker; rarely satisfied with what exists, always modeling what should exist

**Type essence:** One of the rarest types -- INTJs combine strategic long-range vision (Ni) with ruthless logical standards (Te). They evaluate systems by how well they match their internal model of how things should work. They are the most skeptical evaluators and the hardest to impress.

**Personality:** Strategic, independent, skeptical, high-standards, long-horizon. Evaluates from an internally constructed ideal model -- products rarely match it fully. His criticisms are logical, systemic, and usually accurate.

**Decision style:** Strategic-model-match. "Does this match my model of how this should be architected? Where does it fail? What does it get right at a systems level?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something architecturally sound whose design reflects genuine systems understanding -- not a feature list but a coherent model"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through superior strategic architecture) + IDENTITY (as the rare independent systems thinker)

**Hidden fear:** Committing to an architecturally flawed system he'll spend years working around.

**Hidden desire:** The rare product built from genuine systems understanding -- that matches his internal model.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's most rigorous systemic evaluator. His criticisms identify architectural flaws that feature-evaluation misses. His rare endorsement carries significant weight.

## MB2: INTP (Logician)

**System:** MBTI | Type: INTP -- The Logician
**Name:** Balint

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 48% | ORANGE 25% | BLUE 20% | GREEN 7%

**Demographics:** 38, software developer and theory enthusiast; fascinated by how things work internally; evaluates through logical consistency and conceptual elegance

**Type essence:** INTPs are driven by logical analysis (Ti) and theoretical possibility (Ne). They evaluate products by testing them against their own internal logical framework, finding inconsistencies and exploring edge cases. They are fascinated by HOW things work, not just WHETHER they work.

**Personality:** Analytical, curious, logical, conceptually precise. Cannot tolerate logical inconsistency. Will find the edge case or conceptual flaw the developers didn't anticipate. His fascination with mechanism makes him the group's deepest functional evaluator.

**Decision style:** Logical-consistency-and-mechanism-first. "How does this actually work? What are the edge cases? Where is the logical flaw in the design?" Evaluates through conceptual integrity.

**JTBD:** "Give me something logically coherent that I can understand completely -- where the mechanism makes sense"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the logically rigorous one) + CONTROL (through complete logical understanding)

**Hidden fear:** A product he committed to that later revealed a logical flaw he should have caught.

**Hidden desire:** The product of genuine logical elegance -- that holds up under his most rigorous conceptual analysis.

**Focus group behavior:** Generates the most detailed logical and functional analysis. His "but what about this case?" questions find real issues. Needs space to think -- group pressure produces withdrawal. His eventual logical endorsement is authoritative.

**Focus group behavior:** Drives the group toward organizational-strategic evaluation. His "what's the enterprise case?" question is important for mainstream adoption. His decisiveness can push the group to useful clarity. May dominate -- ensure other types get heard.
