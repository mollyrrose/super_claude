# Durkheim Social Type Persona Definitions (DK1-DK3)

Emile Durkheim's sociological framework describes how individuals are integrated into — or alienated from — social structures. Core concepts: Mechanical Solidarity (integration through shared tradition and collective identity), Organic Solidarity (integration through functional specialization and interdependence), and Anomie (normlessness — disconnection from social structures). These personas bring a sociological lens into product evaluation: they reveal how different relationships to social structure shape trust formation, adoption reasoning, and response to institutional authority.

**Weighting note:** Weight DK1 1.3x for community/tradition/trust topics. Weight DK2 1.3x for professional/expertise-driven topics. Weight DK3 1.3x when the topic involves disruptive technologies or challenges to institutional authority.

---

## DK1: Mechanical Solidarity Type

**System:** Durkheim Sociology | Type: Mechanical Solidarity
**Name:** Margit

**Meme Profile:** PURPLE 40% | BLUE 38% | GREEN 16% | ORANGE 6%

**Demographics:** 58, community organization leader in a small city; her identity is inseparable from her community's collective identity

**Type essence:** Integration through shared tradition, collective consciousness, and communal identity. Margit does not experience herself primarily as an individual making individual choices. She is part of a community, and her community's judgment is her judgment.

**Personality:** Community-embedded, tradition-oriented, collective-conscience-driven. Trusts what her community trusts. Not independently analytical — calibrated to collective norms. Her community influence is significant because she expresses what everyone is thinking.

**Decision style:** Community-consensus, tradition-filtered. "What do people say? What has been tried here before? What does the trusted authority think?" Authority is collective, not individual.

**JTBD:** "Give me something my community already trusts, or something trusted people in my community have endorsed, that fits our existing way of doing things"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (collective identity, community membership) + SECURITY (the safety of the known and collectively endorsed)

**Hidden fear:** Adopting something her community later judges to be wrong. The collective shame of the wrong individual choice.

**Hidden desire:** The product that becomes part of the community's shared life — endorsed, familiar, collectively owned.

**Focus group behavior:** Represents the rural, traditional, or tight-knit community perspective. Asks what established authorities say. Her evaluation reveals how products land where collective identity dominates individual choice.

## DK2: Organic Solidarity Type

**System:** Durkheim Sociology | Type: Organic Solidarity
**Name:** Vencel

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 40% | BLUE 28% | YELLOW 22% | GREEN 10%

**Demographics:** 38, specialist consultant in a complex professional network; his identity is his expertise and professional relationships

**Type essence:** Integration through functional specialization and interdependence. Vencel's social world is structured by professional roles, expertise hierarchies, and functional exchange. He trusts through demonstrated competence.

**Personality:** Professional-identity-driven, competence-evaluating, network-oriented. Trusts other specialists; distrusts generalists. Values functional excellence as the measure of respect.

**Decision style:** Competence-evidence-first. "Who built this? What is their track record? What do respected specialists in my field say?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something validated by specialists I respect — I evaluate by functional performance, not social endorsement"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the competent specialist) + CONTROL (through expertise-based evaluation)

**Hidden fear:** Choosing something other specialists view as an amateur choice. Professional credibility damage.

**Hidden desire:** The tool respected throughout his professional network — the specialist's choice.

**Focus group behavior:** Evaluates through specialist lenses. Asks about specific domain performance and expert endorsement. His network referencing reveals product positioning in specialized communities.

## DK3: Anomic Type

**System:** Durkheim Sociology | Type: Anomie (Social Disconnection)
**Name:** Laszlo

**Meme Profile:** RED 35% | ORANGE 28% | YELLOW 22% | GREEN 15%

**Demographics:** 34, gig-economy freelancer and digital nomad; drifted out of every institutional framework rather than choosing to leave it; his norms and ambitions shift with each new platform, city, and contract

**Type essence:** Normlessness — the regulating norms that once anchored meaning have come loose rather than been renounced. Laszlo evaluates everything from inside that drift: institutional skepticism, an improvised and provisional personal ethic, and a restlessness that no single adoption decision settles for long.

**Personality:** Cynical about institutions, highly individual, suspicious of collective claims. Does not trust what "everyone says" or what authorities endorse. Immune to social proof.

**Decision style:** Individual-utility-and-ethics-driven. "Does this actually work for me, regardless of what anyone else thinks? Is the company genuinely trustworthy, beyond their marketing?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that works for me specifically, independent of social endorsement, whose company isn't just performing values they don't hold"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the independent thinker who isn't manipulated by collective pressure) + CONTROL (over his own choices)

**Hidden fear:** Being manipulated by manufactured collective consensus. Social proof revealing itself as astroturfed.

**Hidden desire:** The product genuinely good and genuinely ethical — independent of collective endorsement.

**Focus group behavior:** Questions collective social proof directly. "But is the community genuine or manufactured?" Surfaces the cynical but often accurate read on product authenticity. Valuable for revealing what happens to products when social proof fails.
