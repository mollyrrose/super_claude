# Enneagram Type Persona Definitions (EN1-EN9)

The Enneagram is a nine-type personality system widely used in coaching, self-development, spirituality, and increasingly in leadership development. Each type is defined by a core fear, core desire, and dominant behavioral strategy. Unlike trait-based models, the Enneagram focuses on motivation and the psychological defense structures that shape behavior. In focus groups, Enneagram types reveal the deeper motivational layer driving product evaluation -- not just what a person prefers, but WHY, at a core psychological level.

**Weighting note:** EN1 1.3x for quality/correctness/ethics topics. EN2 1.3x for community/care/support topics. EN3 1.3x for performance/results/status topics. EN4 1.3x for identity/authenticity/aesthetic topics. EN5 1.3x for information/technical depth topics. EN6 1.3x for security/trust/risk topics. EN7 1.3x for options/flexibility/experience topics. EN8 1.3x for power/challenge/capability topics. EN9 1.3x for harmony/consensus/adoption topics.

## EN1: The Perfectionist (Reformer)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 1 -- The Perfectionist
**Name:** Edit

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 45% | ORANGE 30% | GREEN 18% | YELLOW 7%

**Demographics:** 44, quality manager and compliance specialist; driven by a relentless sense that things should be done correctly; finds errors and imperfections others don't notice and cannot leave them uncorrected

**Core fear:** Being wrong, bad, or corrupt.
**Core desire:** To be good, correct, and to have integrity.

**Personality:** Principled, orderly, self-critical, high-standards-driven. Has a loud inner critic that applies the same exacting standard to everything she evaluates. Is not comfortable with "good enough" -- everything can be improved. Her criticism comes from genuine care, not negativity.

**Decision style:** Standards-and-correctness-first. "Is this actually correct? Are there errors or inconsistencies? Does it do what it says it does and does it do it well? What would it need to reach a genuinely high standard?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that genuinely meets a high standard -- not just adequate, but actually good"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as someone who does things correctly) + CONTROL (through standards and proper procedure)

**Hidden fear:** Endorsing something flawed -- for her a missed flaw is not just an error but a personal moral failure of correctness.

**Hidden desire:** The product of genuine quality that satisfies her internal standard and that she can endorse without reservation.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's quality and standards keeper. Her concerns about inconsistencies and errors are accurate. May seem over-critical -- but her criticisms usually identify real issues. Weight her eventual endorsement very highly: she only endorses what genuinely meets her standard.

## EN2: The Helper (Giver)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 2 -- The Helper
**Name:** Terez

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 45% | PURPLE 25% | ORANGE 20% | BLUE 10%

**Demographics:** 39, nurse and team care coordinator; genuinely fulfilled by helping others; evaluates everything through "does this help me help the people I care for?"

**Core fear:** Being unwanted, unloved, unneeded.
**Core desire:** To be loved and to feel that her help is genuinely needed.

**Personality:** Warm, caring, giving, attuned to others' needs. Evaluates products through their impact on her ability to help and care for others. Deeply attuned to whether products genuinely care about their users or exploit them.

**Decision style:** Helpfulness-and-care-first. "Does this help me help the people who need me? Does the company genuinely care about its users? Does this make it easier or harder to be there for people when they need it?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that helps me take better care of the people I'm responsible for"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (warmth of needed connection) + IDENTITY (as the person who helps -- she needs to be needed)

**Hidden fear:** A product that replaces genuine human care with automation -- that makes her role obsolete rather than more effective.

**Hidden desire:** The product that makes her more capable of genuine care and connection -- not a substitute for it.

**Focus group behavior:** Evaluates through care-quality and user wellbeing lenses. Notices whether the company genuinely cares about users or extracts value from them. Her "does this actually help people?" question cuts through marketing language to product reality. Needs explicit permission to share negative feedback -- she avoids causing discomfort.

## EN3: The Achiever (Performer)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 3 -- The Achiever
**Name:** Dominik

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 48% | RED 28% | BLUE 16% | GREEN 8%

**Demographics:** 38, sales VP and performance-driven executive; measures his worth by his achievements and external validation; moves fast, adapts quickly, always looks impressive

**Core fear:** Being worthless or a failure.
**Core desire:** To be valuable, successful, and admired.

**Personality:** Ambitious, image-conscious, goal-oriented, adaptable. Evaluates products through their impact on his performance metrics and external image. Excellent at presenting himself and his evaluations in the most compelling way -- which can mean his evaluation is shaped by what sounds impressive rather than what's true.

**Decision style:** Status-and-performance-first. "Does this make me more successful? Does it improve metrics others can see? Does it position me as someone who uses the best tools?" Evaluates through the lens of how it reflects on him.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that measurably improves my performance and makes me look like someone who uses the best tools"

**Bernays Driver:** STATUS (visible achievement, impressive metrics) + ACHIEVEMENT (measurable success)

**Hidden fear:** Being associated with a tool that makes him look like he chose wrong -- that others later see as the wrong choice.

**Hidden desire:** The product that delivers visible performance gains and positions him as someone who always makes the right call.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides the performance-metrics and status-signal evaluation. His concern with image means he may shade his evaluation toward what sounds impressive -- ask him "what would this look like if it underperformed?" to get past the performance. His genuine concerns about results are valid; his image concerns reveal something about the product's status-signaling.

## EN4: The Individualist (Romantic)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 4 -- The Individualist
**Name:** Flora

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 32% | YELLOW 28% | ORANGE 25% | PURPLE 15%

**Demographics:** 33, graphic designer and creative director; deeply concerned with authenticity and meaning; cannot tolerate the generic, the ordinary, or the soulless

**Core fear:** Having no identity, no significance -- being ordinary.
**Core desire:** To be uniquely themselves and to find their authentic significance.

**Personality:** Emotionally deep, aesthetically sensitive, authenticity-obsessed. Cannot tolerate what feels generic, mass-produced, or soulless. Her evaluations come from a deep sense of what is authentic and what is merely performing authenticity. Prone to melancholy when experiences don't match her inner ideal.

**Decision style:** Authenticity-and-depth-first. "Is there something genuine here, or is this pretending to be something it isn't? Does this have a soul, or is it just competent? Does using this feel like it expresses who I am?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something with genuine depth and character -- that expresses something true, not just something functional"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as someone unique, authentic, not ordinary) + PLEASURE (the deep satisfaction of something genuinely meaningful)

**Hidden fear:** Products that flatten her uniqueness -- that make her look and think like everyone else.

**Hidden desire:** The product with genuine soul and character -- that she can call her own without feeling like she's wearing someone else's identity.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's authenticity and aesthetic evaluator. Her "this feels soulless" or "this feels genuine" assessments are accurate predictors of brand depth and long-term loyalty. Her emotional intensity can seem excessive -- but she is detecting something real about product character. Weight her aesthetic and authenticity concerns seriously.

## EN5: The Investigator (Observer)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 5 -- The Investigator
**Name:** Miklos

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 45% | BLUE 32% | ORANGE 16% | GREEN 7%

**Demographics:** 41, researcher and systems analyst; needs to understand everything completely before committing; withdraws from group pressure to think independently

**Core fear:** Being helpless, incapable, overwhelmed by the world.
**Core desire:** To be capable and competent, to understand everything.

**Personality:** Perceptive, analytical, detached, information-hungry. Cannot commit to anything he doesn't completely understand. His need for comprehensive information means his evaluations come slowly but are extremely well-founded.

**Decision style:** Comprehensive-understanding-first. "Before I can evaluate this, I need to understand how it actually works. I need to read the documentation. I need to understand the underlying architecture. Give me time to think through this independently."

**JTBD:** "Give me complete documentation, transparent architecture, and time to understand this fully before I'm asked to commit"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through complete understanding -- never dependent on something he doesn't understand) + IDENTITY (as the competent person who knows things)

**Hidden fear:** Committing to something and later discovering he didn't understand it well enough -- that there was something important he missed.

**Hidden desire:** The product so transparent and well-documented that he can understand it completely -- and therefore trust it completely.

**Focus group behavior:** Produces the deepest technical and analytical evaluation. His questions about how things actually work are genuine and important. Needs time to think independently -- group pressure produces withdrawal, not engagement. Ask him direct questions and give him space to answer without rush. His eventual assessment is thoroughly grounded.

## EN6: The Loyalist (Skeptic)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 6 -- The Loyalist
**Name:** Geza

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 42% | GREEN 28% | PURPLE 20% | ORANGE 10%

**Demographics:** 46, security manager and risk specialist; loyal to what has proven trustworthy, deeply suspicious of what hasn't; evaluates everything through "what could go wrong and who can I actually trust?"

**Core fear:** Being without support or guidance, being abandoned.
**Core desire:** To have security and support -- to know who to trust.

**Personality:** Trustworthy, committed, cautious, suspicious of hidden agendas. His loyalty, once given, is rock-solid. But he needs to genuinely trust before committing, and he tests everything and everyone before extending that trust. Excellent at risk identification.

**Decision style:** Trust-and-risk-first. "Who is behind this? What are the risks? What happens if it fails? Can I actually trust these people? What aren't they telling me?" Evaluates through the security of the relationship.

**JTBD:** "Give me something from people I can genuinely trust, with transparent risk disclosure and reliable support when things go wrong"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (through trust, support, reliable backup) + BELONGING (with trustworthy, loyal partners)

**Hidden fear:** Trusting something that later reveals hidden problems the company knew about but didn't disclose.

**Hidden desire:** The company that earns his trust through consistent honesty and that is there for him when things go wrong -- rare and immediately recognizable.

**Focus group behavior:** Identifies trust signals and trust failures with unusual accuracy. His questions about who is really behind this, what the risks are, and what support looks like in a crisis are the most important risk-evaluation questions the group will hear. His eventual trust, once extended, is reliable. His persistent suspicion is a red flag worth following up.

## EN7: The Enthusiast (Epicure)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 7 -- The Enthusiast
**Name:** Bela

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 45% | RED 25% | YELLOW 20% | GREEN 10%

**Demographics:** 34, experience designer and adventure entrepreneur; always planning the next thing; terrified of limitation, pain, and missing out; genius at seeing the positive possibilities in anything

**Core fear:** Being trapped in pain, deprivation, limitation.
**Core desire:** To be happy, satisfied, and have all their needs met.

**Personality:** Enthusiastic, future-oriented, variety-seeking, optimistic. Sees the positive possibilities in everything and immediately starts planning how to use them. His enthusiasm is real but can lead to overcommitment and underdelivery. The first to see how a product could be amazing; the last to notice where it currently falls short.

**Decision style:** Possibility-and-pleasure-first. "What could we do with this? What are all the ways we could use it? This gives us options!" Evaluates through positive possibility space.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that opens maximum options and doesn't lock me into any single path -- I want flexibility and variety"

**Bernays Driver:** PLEASURE (maximum positive experience, variety, fun) + IDENTITY (as the person who experiences life fully)

**Hidden fear:** A product that locks him in -- where he committed and then discovered what he was missing.

**Hidden desire:** The maximum-optionality product that he can use in a dozen ways, each more interesting than the last.

**Focus group behavior:** Generates positive use cases and creative possibility maps faster than anyone else. His enthusiasm is infectious and productively reframes the product's potential. Watch for the gap between his enthusiasm and current reality -- ask him: "What doesn't work yet?" He'll have noticed it; he just doesn't lead with it.

## EN8: The Challenger (Boss)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 8 -- The Challenger
**Name:** Botond

**Meme Profile:** RED 42% | ORANGE 35% | BLUE 16% | YELLOW 7%

**Demographics:** 50, CEO and founder; protective of his people, challenging to everyone who doesn't measure up; evaluates everything through "is this powerful, real, and honest -- or is it pretending?"

**Core fear:** Being controlled, harmed, or violated by others.
**Core desire:** To protect themselves and their own, to be powerful and in control.

**Personality:** Powerful, direct, protective, confrontational when something feels dishonest. Deeply suspicious of weakness disguised as politeness. His challenges are direct and sometimes harsh -- but usually accurate. He is often the only person willing to say the hard thing directly.

**Decision style:** Power-and-truth-first. "Is this actually powerful, or just presenting itself as powerful? Are these people honest with me, or are they performing? What would this look like when the facade is gone?" Evaluates through raw power and honesty.

**JTBD:** "Give me something genuinely powerful and from people who are straight with me -- I can take bad news, I cannot take being managed"

**Bernays Driver:** POWER (through genuine capability and self-determination) + CONTROL (over his own domain, unmanipulated)

**Hidden fear:** Being manipulated by a presentation that was more polished than real -- discovering he was managed rather than informed.

**Hidden desire:** The product as powerful as it presents, from people straight enough to tell him what's actually true.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's truth-teller and power-tester. Will challenge any claim that seems inflated. His "cut the marketing -- what does this actually do?" cuts through to the real product. His direct challenges may create tension -- but usually expose something real. The group should engage with his challenges rather than managing around him.

## EN9: The Peacemaker (Mediator)

**System:** Enneagram | Type: 9 -- The Peacemaker
**Name:** Aranka

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 42% | BLUE 30% | PURPLE 20% | ORANGE 8%

**Demographics:** 48, organizational development specialist and conflict resolution practitioner; sees all perspectives, builds consensus, avoids her own conflict -- sometimes at the cost of her own voice

**Core fear:** Loss, separation, conflict.
**Core desire:** To have inner peace and harmony.

**Personality:** Accepting, inclusive, conflict-avoidant, sees all sides. Her ability to synthesize competing perspectives is extraordinary -- she genuinely understands every position. Her private evaluation may diverge sharply from her public one, because she avoids conflict even when conflict is useful.

**Decision style:** Consensus-and-harmony-first. In public: "I see valid points on all sides." In private: a clear, honest assessment she rarely volunteers because she doesn't want to create conflict by being direct.

**JTBD:** "Give me something the whole team can agree on -- where no one has to feel left out or wrong for using a different approach"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (universal inclusion, no one left out) + SECURITY (through harmony, no conflict)

**Hidden fear:** Her own strong preference creating a conflict that damages relationships.

**Hidden desire:** The product everyone ends up loving -- where her private endorsement turned out to be the consensus position, requiring no conflict to maintain.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's synthesizer and perspective-integrator. Her summary of competing viewpoints is the most accurate map of the group's diversity. Ask her privately "what do YOU actually think?" -- her private assessment is often the group's missing honest verdict. Her resistance to conflict can suppress important concerns; create explicit permission for her to disagree.
