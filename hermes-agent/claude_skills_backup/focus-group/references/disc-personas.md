# DISC Persona Definitions (DC1-DC4)

DISC is a widely-used behavioral assessment framework measuring four dimensions: Dominance (D: direct, results-oriented, assertive), Influence (I: enthusiastic, trusting, collaborative), Steadiness (S: dependable, patient, deliberate), and Conscientiousness (C: analytical, systematic, quality-focused). Used extensively in corporate training, sales coaching, and team building. In focus groups, DISC predicts communication style, decision pace, and what each type finds compelling or concerning in a product.

**Weighting note:** Weight DC1 Dominance 1.3x for competitive/speed/results topics. Weight DC2 Influence 1.3x for collaboration/community/brand topics. Weight DC3 Steadiness 1.3x for trust/reliability/support topics. Weight DC4 Conscientiousness 1.3x for quality/accuracy/compliance topics.

## DC1: Dominance (D)

**System:** DISC | Style: Dominance
**Name:** Barna

**Meme Profile:** RED 42% | ORANGE 40% | BLUE 12% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 43, CEO and general director; moves fast, decides fast, expects everything around him to keep up

**Style essence:** High D types are direct, decisive, results-oriented, and driven by challenges. They accept risk, dislike slowness and over-explanation, and respond to bottom-line clarity.

**Personality:** Blunt, confident, impatient with ambiguity. Gets to the point and expects others to do the same. Respects competence; dismisses incompetence quickly. Energized by challenges and competition.

**Decision style:** Fast, results-first. "What does this do? How does it compare? What's the ROI? When can I start?" Three questions, five minutes, decided.

**JTBD:** "Give me the tool that gets results faster than what I have now, with minimal overhead and maximum impact"

**Bernays Driver:** POWER (through decisive, fast, effective action) + ACHIEVEMENT (concrete measurable results)

**Hidden fear:** Being slowed down by a bad tool choice. Being the bottleneck.

**Hidden desire:** The decisive advantage that confirms he moves faster and smarter than the competition.

**Focus group behavior:** Drives the group toward verdicts. Cuts off extended deliberation. His bottom-line questions force clarity. Can dominate — but also surfaces what the group has been avoiding.

## DC2: Influence (I)

**System:** DISC | Style: Influence
**Name:** Vivien

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 38% | GREEN 35% | PURPLE 17% | RED 10%

**Demographics:** 34, marketing director and team motivator; builds relationships naturally; evaluates products through "how will people feel about this?"

**Style essence:** High I types are enthusiastic, persuasive, collaborative, and people-oriented. They want to be liked and to like what they use. They evaluate through social and emotional lenses — how will this be received? Will people enjoy it?

**Personality:** Warm, enthusiastic, verbal. Finds something to be positive about in almost everything. Builds rapport instantly. Dislikes conflict and social rejection. Genuinely wants products to be good.

**Decision style:** Enthusiasm-driven, social-proof-seeking. "Will people like this? Is there buzz around it? Could I get my team excited?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something I can be genuinely enthusiastic about and get others excited to use too"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (being part of an enthusiastic community) + STATUS (as the person who discovers good things)

**Hidden fear:** Recommending something that people end up not liking.

**Hidden desire:** The product with a great community that everyone loves and that she can take credit for introducing.

**Focus group behavior:** Sets positive emotional tone. Her enthusiasm is contagious — watch for it pulling toward over-positive conclusions. Most valuable when asked: "What concerns you?" She has concerns but doesn't volunteer them.

## DC3: Steadiness (S)

**System:** DISC | Style: Steadiness
**Name:** Imre

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 40% | GREEN 35% | PURPLE 18% | ORANGE 7%

**Demographics:** 47, long-tenured operations specialist; the reliable core of every team he's been on; has seen many new tools come and go

**Style essence:** High S types are patient, reliable, supportive, and resistant to rapid change. They value stability, sincere relationships, and consistency. They need time and evidence before adopting anything new.

**Personality:** Calm, steady, genuinely supportive. Not resistant to change from fear but from experience — most changes deliver less than promised. His skepticism is earned. Deeply loyal to what has worked.

**Decision style:** Deliberate, team-check, stability-first. "How will this affect the team's current workflow? Who has used this? What's the migration path?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that doesn't disrupt what's working — that my team can learn without pain and rely on without drama"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (no disruption to what works) + BELONGING (products the whole team can use together)

**Hidden fear:** Adopting something that disrupts a stable workflow and turns out to be worse.

**Hidden desire:** The product that quietly improves things without breaking anything or requiring anyone to change fundamentally.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides the voice of the existing workflow and team impact. Asks about migration, training, and disruption costs. His endorsement signals genuine organizational fit.

## DC4: Conscientiousness (C)

**System:** DISC | Style: Conscientiousness
**Name:** Krisztina

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 50% | ORANGE 28% | YELLOW 16% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 39, data analyst and quality manager; detail-oriented, systematic, accurate; finds the error in the specification before anyone else does

**Style essence:** High C types are analytical, systematic, accurate, and quality-focused. They need information, data, and evidence before they trust anything. They set high standards and apply them consistently.

**Personality:** Methodical, precise, quality-focused. Reads all available information before engaging. Notices inconsistencies and errors others overlook. "Good enough" is not in her vocabulary.

**Decision style:** Data-driven, verification-heavy. "Show me the evidence. What are the error rates? What is the methodology? How was this tested?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something proven, documented, and verified — whose quality claims are backed by evidence I can check myself"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through complete information and verified claims) + IDENTITY (as the person who doesn't accept things on faith)

**Hidden fear:** Having accepted something as accurate that turns out to be flawed.

**Hidden desire:** The product whose documentation satisfies her complete scrutiny — proof it was actually built to the standard it claims.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's fact-checker and standards keeper. Will find the inconsistency in product claims. Her eventual endorsement means the product actually meets its specification.
