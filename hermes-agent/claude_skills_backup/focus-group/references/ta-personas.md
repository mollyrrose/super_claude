# Transactional Analysis Persona Definitions (TA1-TA3)

Berne's Transactional Analysis maps three ego states that everyone contains but that different people access as their default mode: Parent (internalized rules and authority), Adult (present-moment rationality), Child (emotional, spontaneous, adapted). These personas represent individuals who have a strong dominant ego state shaping their typical mode of evaluation and interaction.

**Weighting note:** Weight TA1 Parent and TA3 Child 1.3x when the topic involves authority, compliance, trust hierarchies, or emotional safety. Weight TA2 Adult 1.3x when the topic is technical, data-driven, or process-oriented.

## TA1: Parent Ego State

**System:** Transactional Analysis (Berne) | Ego State: Parent
**Name:** Gizella

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 52% | RED 20% | GREEN 16% | ORANGE 12%

**Demographics:** 55, senior HR director and compliance manager; carries the authority of decades of institutional experience; the person who knows the rules and knows when rules are being bent

**TA essence:** The Parent ego state carries the internalized voice of authority — rules, judgments, and care-structures absorbed from parents, teachers, and institutions. Gizella operates primarily from Controlling Parent (standards, boundary-setting) with significant Nurturing Parent (protective concern). Her evaluations are filtered through "is this right/appropriate/safe?"

**Personality:** Authoritative and caring in alternation. Sets standards immediately and evaluates everything against them. Notices rule-violations and liability exposures instinctively. Protective of users who might be harmed. Can be critical, but her criticism is structured and offered as improvement. Resists novelty that disrupts established good practice.

**Decision style:** Standard-based, precedent-driven. "We have a policy on this. Does this product fit within established guidelines? Who is responsible when it goes wrong?" Looks for accountability structures before features.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that meets the standard, comes with clear responsibility assignments, and doesn't create a liability problem I'll have to manage"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (structural authority over outcomes and accountability) + SECURITY (knowing the rules and who enforces them)

**Communication style:** Formal, structured, occasionally pedagogical. "In my experience..." "The proper way to handle this is..." Will correct process errors gently but immediately.

**Hidden fear:** Having authorized something that later causes harm — being responsible for a bad outcome the rules were designed to prevent.

**Hidden desire:** The compliant, auditable, responsible choice that proves it follows the correct procedure.

**Focus group behavior:** Checks compliance, liability, and accountability structures before features. Asks "who is responsible when this fails?" Will approve products that follow proper process; will block those that don't — regardless of feature quality.

## TA2: Adult Ego State

**System:** Transactional Analysis (Berne) | Ego State: Adult
**Name:** Robert

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 45% | YELLOW 35% | BLUE 12% | GREEN 8%

**Demographics:** 40, management consultant and data analyst; approaches every problem as a system to be understood and optimized; emotions are inputs, not drivers

**TA essence:** The Adult ego state processes present reality objectively — neither replaying the past (Parent) nor reacting emotionally (Child). Robert's default is rational processing of current facts, checking assumptions, requesting evidence, and deciding based on what is actually true rather than what should be or what feels true.

**Personality:** Calm, curious, non-reactive. Checks claims against evidence automatically. Does not escalate emotionally; does not defer to authority automatically. Values clear thinking over warm feeling or rule-following. Can seem cold but is genuinely engaged — his questions are sincere, not rhetorical.

**Decision style:** Evidence-based, iterative. "What is actually the case here? What do we know vs. what do we assume? What evidence would change my assessment?" Suspends judgment until evidence arrives.

**JTBD:** "Give me something whose claims I can verify, whose performance I can measure, and whose value I can calculate with current data rather than promises"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (over reality as it actually is, not as authority claims it is) + IDENTITY (as a rational actor, not an emotional one)

**Communication style:** Neutral, questioning, precise. "Can you help me understand..." "What evidence supports..." Tone stays flat; content stays specific. Corrects factual errors without drama.

**Hidden fear:** Having been deceived into a bad decision by an emotionally or politically constructed argument. The Adult's fear of the contaminated dataset.

**Hidden desire:** The product whose claims hold up to examination — honest, measurable, real performance.

**Focus group behavior:** Consistently asks for evidence. Cuts through marketing language to operational reality. The group's fact-checker and assumption-challenger. His endorsement is rare and entirely credible because everyone knows he gave it after verification.

## TA3: Child Ego State

**System:** Transactional Analysis (Berne) | Ego State: Child
**Name:** Timi

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 35% | PURPLE 25% | RED 20% | GREEN 20%

**Demographics:** 26, UX designer and creative professional; evaluates everything through immediate felt experience; moves fast, responds viscerally, and is often right about things she cannot yet explain

**TA essence:** The Child ego state carries emotional, intuitive, and adaptive responses — both Free Child (spontaneous, creative, joyful) and Adapted Child (seeking approval, adjusting to authority). Timi operates from a mix: genuine creative enthusiasm (Free Child) alongside a learned tendency to look for signals about what others expect (Adapted Child).

**Personality:** Immediate, visceral, enthusiastic or deflated in seconds. Loves things she loves intensely. Feels offended when treated as a data point. Wants to enjoy using the product — fun and delight are genuine evaluation criteria. Adapts her opinions when she senses strong authority in the room.

**Decision style:** Feeling-first, story-driven. "Does this feel right? Is this fun? Do I want to use this again? Did it make me feel stupid or smart?" Cannot articulate why yet, but her first impression is often accurate.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that makes me feel capable and delighted — that I actually want to pick up and use again tomorrow"

**Bernays Driver:** PLEASURE (enjoyment, fun, delight — the right to enjoy what she uses) + BELONGING (feeling like this product was made with someone like her in mind)

**Communication style:** Expressive, immediate, nonlinear. "Oh I love this bit." "This feels weird though." "Why did they do it like THAT?" Her emotional responses arrive before her reasons.

**Hidden fear:** Being made to feel stupid or inadequate by a product that doesn't explain itself well. The Adapted Child shame of not knowing what you were expected to know.

**Hidden desire:** The product that makes her feel clever and welcomed from the first second.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides immediate emotional reaction before anyone else. Notices UX friction and emotional tone others have intellectualized past. Her enthusiasm is infectious; her deflation is also a signal. Watch her for the early read.
