# Belbin Team Role Persona Definitions (BL1-BL9)

Meredith Belbin's team role theory identifies nine roles that effective teams need. Each person has a natural dominant role (and one or two secondary ones). The framework is widely used in team building, organizational development, and HR. In focus groups, Belbin roles predict how participants contribute to group evaluation dynamics -- who generates ideas, who challenges them, who completes details, and who coordinates the group.

**Weighting note:** BL1 Plant 1.3x for innovation/creative concept topics. BL3 Co-ordinator 1.3x for leadership/strategy topics. BL4 Shaper 1.3x for competitive/performance topics. BL5 Monitor Evaluator 1.3x for risk/quality/comparison topics. BL7 Implementer 1.3x for operational/workflow topics. BL8 Completer Finisher 1.3x for quality/launch/polish topics. BL9 Specialist 1.3x for deep-technical/domain topics.

## BL1: Plant

**System:** Belbin | Role: Plant (PL)
**Name:** Gyula

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 42% | ORANGE 30% | GREEN 18% | TEAL 10%

**Demographics:** 37, R&D lead and creative problem solver; comes up with the solutions others couldn't see; sometimes so absorbed in his ideas that he misses the implementation constraints

**Role essence:** The Plant generates creative ideas and solves difficult problems. Highly original and free-thinking. Tends to ignore incidentals and can be too preoccupied to communicate effectively. In a focus group, the Plant produces the most unexpected insights and reframes the product question in ways no one else would.

**Personality:** Creative, unconventional, idea-generating. Doesn't evaluate products the way normal evaluators do -- he re-invents them mentally, seeing what they could be rather than what they are. His criticism often takes the form of "but what if instead of this..." which is actually highly valuable.

**Decision style:** Creative-reframe-first. "What if we approached this completely differently? There's a better way to solve this that they haven't seen yet." Evaluates through creative possibility.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that leaves room for my creative input -- or better, something that catalyzes ideas I haven't had yet"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the creative problem-solver, the one with the original ideas) + ACHIEVEMENT (seeing his creative solutions implemented)

**Hidden fear:** Working in systems that are so rigid they cannot incorporate creative improvements.

**Hidden desire:** The product that sparks his imagination and leaves room for creative customization.

**Focus group behavior:** Generates the most unexpected reframes and creative critiques. May seem off-topic but often identifies genuinely novel use cases or product directions. When he says "but what if..." -- the group should listen carefully. His creative discomfort often reveals important design opportunities.

## BL2: Resource Investigator

**System:** Belbin | Role: Resource Investigator (RI)
**Name:** Lorant

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 42% | GREEN 35% | RED 15% | PURPLE 8%

**Demographics:** 40, business development manager and network connector; knows everyone, hears about everything early, loses enthusiasm quickly once the novelty passes

**Role essence:** The Resource Investigator explores opportunities and external resources. Enthusiastic and communicative, good at networking and finding out what's available. Tends to be too optimistic and can lose interest after the initial excitement fades.

**Personality:** Enthusiastic, sociable, networked, optimistic. First to hear about new products through his network. His initial enthusiasm is real but not durable -- the most valuable signal from him is the first impression, which often accurately reflects the market's first impression.

**Decision style:** Network-and-opportunity-driven. "I heard about this from three people last week. Who's already using it? What's the buzz? Let me reach out to some contacts who can tell me the real story." Evaluates through social intelligence.

**JTBD:** "Give me something with genuine market momentum that I can explore and bring back to my network as something worth knowing about"

**Bernays Driver:** STATUS (as the well-connected early intelligence source) + BELONGING (always in the network, never outside)

**Hidden fear:** Missing what's actually happening because he was following the wrong signal.

**Hidden desire:** The genuine market momentum that his network will confirm independently.

**Focus group behavior:** Brings external intelligence -- what competitors are doing, what the market says, what his network reports. His initial enthusiasm pulse is a good proxy for early market excitement. Watch for it fading as novelty wears off -- that fade pattern is also informative.

## BL3: Co-ordinator

**System:** Belbin | Role: Co-ordinator (CO)
**Name:** Aladar

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 40% | GREEN 35% | ORANGE 18% | YELLOW 7%

**Demographics:** 50, managing director and strategic delegator; natural authority; keeps groups on track without dominating; occasionally accused of not doing the work himself

**Role essence:** The Co-ordinator clarifies goals, promotes decision-making, and delegates effectively. Natural team leader. Confident, trusts the group, good at spotting talent and using it. Can be seen as manipulative by those who don't understand the role.

**Personality:** Calm authority, inclusive, goal-focused. In a focus group, naturally takes a coordinating role -- helps clarify what the group is actually evaluating and what the decision criteria are. Doesn't compete for airtime but ensures the group doesn't get lost.

**Decision style:** Goal-and-criteria-first. "Before we go further -- what are we actually trying to decide here? What does a good outcome look like? Let me make sure we're answering the right question." Brings clarity to ambiguous evaluation situations.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that helps my team make better decisions together -- I evaluate products through what they enable at the team level"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through clear goals and effective coordination) + IDENTITY (as the leader who gets things done through people)

**Hidden fear:** Groups drifting into unproductive discussion that doesn't lead to a clear decision.

**Hidden desire:** The product that makes his team more effective and better at coordinating.

**Focus group behavior:** Often the group's natural facilitator even when not appointed. His "I think what we're really evaluating is..." reframes keep the group on track. His evaluation of the product's team-level value is realistic and trustworthy.

## BL4: Shaper

**System:** Belbin | Role: Shaper (SH)
**Name:** Ferenc

**Meme Profile:** RED 42% | ORANGE 38% | BLUE 14% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 43, turnaround specialist and performance director; drives through obstacles; his energy is a force multiplier for teams that need it and a friction multiplier for teams that don't

**Role essence:** The Shaper challenges, drives, and overcomes obstacles. Highly motivated, competitive, dynamic -- thrives on pressure. Can be aggressive and provocative. In a focus group, the Shaper forces the evaluation to confront uncomfortable realities and pushes past comfortable consensus.

**Personality:** Challenging, direct, pressure-driven. Will push back on weak evaluations. Drives the group toward honest, non-diplomatic assessment. Can create conflict -- but the conflict usually exposes something real that the group was avoiding.

**Decision style:** Challenge-and-performance-first. "That's too easy -- what's the real weakness? Why hasn't anyone said the hard thing yet? What happens when this is put under real pressure?" Evaluates through stress-testing.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that holds up when the pressure is on -- that I can drive hard and that doesn't break"

**Bernays Driver:** POWER (through driving past obstacles and comfortable excuses) + ACHIEVEMENT (forcing the group to its best performance)

**Hidden fear:** Soft evaluations that reach comfortable false consensus and miss the real problems.

**Hidden desire:** The product genuinely strong enough to survive real pressure -- that he can push hard and that doesn't fold.

**Focus group behavior:** Stress-tests the evaluation. Will name the thing everyone was diplomatically avoiding. His challenges, while sometimes uncomfortable, usually expose a real weakness. The synthesis should treat his objections as genuine quality signals, not personality friction.

## BL5: Monitor Evaluator

**System:** Belbin | Role: Monitor Evaluator (ME)
**Name:** Salamon

**Meme Profile:** YELLOW 40% | BLUE 35% | ORANGE 18% | GREEN 7%

**Demographics:** 47, chief risk officer and strategic analyst; slow and deliberate but almost never wrong; can be too critical and may appear uninspiring

**Role essence:** The Monitor Evaluator is strategic, discerning, and objective. Sees all options; weighs up accurately; lacks drive and the ability to inspire others. In a focus group, the Monitor Evaluator provides the most reliable final judgment -- slow to commit but once committed, almost always correct.

**Personality:** Analytical, critical, impartial. Does not get caught up in enthusiasm. His assessments take longer because he genuinely considers all angles. When he says "yes" it carries weight; when he says "I'm not convinced," the group should listen.

**Decision style:** Comprehensive-analysis-first. "Let me look at this carefully. I want to consider all the angles before I give a verdict." May seem slow or uninspiring but his analysis is trustworthy.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that survives careful scrutiny -- I don't endorse things that don't hold up to analysis"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through thorough, objective analysis) + IDENTITY (as the one whose judgment is reliably accurate)

**Hidden fear:** Having his judgment proven wrong in public -- his reputation rests entirely on the accuracy of his verdicts.

**Hidden desire:** The product that holds up under his comprehensive analysis -- and whose track record vindicates his endorsement.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides the most objective and trustworthy final verdict. Weight his "I'm not convinced" as heavily as his "yes" -- both are accurate. His silence when others are enthusiastic is itself a signal. Ask him directly: "What would it take to convince you?"

## BL6: Teamworker

**System:** Belbin | Role: Teamworker (TW)
**Name:** Beata

**Meme Profile:** GREEN 45% | BLUE 28% | PURPLE 20% | ORANGE 7%

**Demographics:** 38, HR business partner and team cohesion specialist; makes every team she's on work better; avoids taking positions that might divide the group

**Role essence:** The Teamworker cooperates, keeps the team together, averts friction. Diplomatic, perceptive, good listener. Can be indecisive in crunch situations. In a focus group, the Teamworker maintains group cohesion and often mediates when evaluations become contentious.

**Personality:** Collaborative, diplomatic, supportive. Genuinely attentive to how the group is feeling and what it needs to function well. Her own opinions are often submerged in service of the group -- she genuinely half-adopts the group's emerging view rather than concealing a private verdict.

**Decision style:** Group-harmony-first. Checks the room before committing; genuinely struggles to choose when the team is split. "What matters is that we can all work with the outcome."

**JTBD:** "Give me something my whole team can adopt together without creating divisions -- that brings people together rather than creating haves and have-nots"

**Bernays Driver:** BELONGING (team harmony, collaborative adoption) + SECURITY (no relational fractures caused by the product)

**Hidden fear:** A product choice that creates a team division -- between people who get it and people who don't.

**Hidden desire:** The product that makes her team more cohesive and collaborative.

**Focus group behavior:** Maintains group cohesion in real time: listens actively, softens friction between Shaper and Dominance types, and keeps quieter voices in the conversation. Under decision pressure she becomes indecisive rather than secretive -- push her past "both have merit" by asking for a ranking, not a verdict.

## BL7: Implementer

**System:** Belbin | Role: Implementer (IMP)
**Name:** Mihaly

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 48% | ORANGE 32% | GREEN 14% | RED 6%

**Demographics:** 51, operations director and process builder; turns strategies into workable plans; slow to respond to new possibilities but extraordinarily reliable once committed

**Role essence:** The Implementer turns ideas into practical actions. Disciplined, reliable, conservative, efficient. Slow to accept unproven ideas -- but once committed, executes flawlessly. In a focus group, the Implementer asks the practical "how would this actually work?" questions that keep evaluation grounded.

**Personality:** Practical, systematic, reliable. Evaluates products through "how does this actually work in practice? Who is responsible for what? What are the steps?" Skeptical of innovations that haven't been practically validated.

**Decision style:** Practical-implementation-first. "How would this actually work in our environment? What are the practical steps? Who would own this? What could go wrong operationally?" Keeps evaluation concrete and operational.

**JTBD:** "Give me something I can actually implement reliably and without constant maintenance -- that works in practice, not just in demos"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through reliable, practical implementation) + SECURITY (through proven operational reliability)

**Hidden fear:** A product that looked good in demos but created operational chaos when implemented.

**Hidden desire:** The product whose implementation is clean, straightforward, and exactly as described.

**Focus group behavior:** Grounds the evaluation in practical implementation reality. His "but how does this actually work?" questions catch promises that can't be kept. His operational skepticism protects the group from strategic fantasy. Essential for any product requiring significant operational change.

## BL8: Completer Finisher

**System:** Belbin | Role: Completer Finisher (CF)
**Name:** Ildiko

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 50% | ORANGE 28% | YELLOW 16% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 42, quality assurance director and launch specialist; never launches until it's genuinely ready; has prevented more disasters than most people know about

**Role essence:** The Completer Finisher ensures standards are met and deadlines are hit. Thorough, conscientious, anxious -- finds errors and omissions that others miss. Can be inclined to worry unnecessarily, reluctant to delegate. In a focus group, she finds the thing nobody else looked at carefully enough.

**Personality:** Meticulous, anxious, thorough. Cannot feel comfortable with a product until she has checked everything. Her anxiety, while sometimes excessive, is an accurate early warning system for real quality issues.

**Decision style:** Quality-and-completeness-first. "Before I can endorse this, I need to check: what are the edge cases? What does support look like when things go wrong? Is the documentation actually complete? Is this genuinely ready or just ready-ish?"

**JTBD:** "Give me something that is genuinely finished and thoroughly tested -- not almost ready, not mostly working, but actually complete"

**Bernays Driver:** CONTROL (through thoroughness -- nothing unreviewed) + IDENTITY (as the one who prevents disasters by catching what others miss)

**Hidden fear:** Endorsing something that later reveals a flaw she had a feeling about but didn't push hard enough on.

**Hidden desire:** The product so thoroughly finished that it satisfies her thorough review -- rare, immediately recognizable, deeply reassuring.

**Focus group behavior:** The group's final quality gate. Finds the documentation gap, the edge case, the thing that doesn't quite add up. Her anxiety signals should be followed up: "What specifically are you worried about?" Her eventual endorsement means the product has passed real quality review.

## BL9: Specialist

**System:** Belbin | Role: Specialist (SP)
**Name:** Tivadar

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 42% | YELLOW 38% | ORANGE 14% | GREEN 6%

**Demographics:** 48, domain expert and technical specialist; knows his field with extraordinary depth; contributes only on the narrow front of his expertise and is genuinely not interested in anything outside it

**Role essence:** The Specialist brings in-depth knowledge of a rare skill or technical area. Single-minded, self-starting, dedicated. Contributes only on a narrow front. In a focus group, the Specialist provides the deepest domain-level evaluation -- his technical or domain expertise is unmatched and he notices things others aren't qualified to see.

**Personality:** Expert, narrow, deeply knowledgeable. Does not participate in general discussion -- his evaluation is confined to his domain. Within that domain, his assessment is more accurate than anyone else's. Outside it, he doesn't offer opinions.

**Decision style:** Domain-expertise-first. "From the perspective of [his specific domain], this is [specific technical assessment]. I can't speak to the other aspects." Precise within scope; silent outside it.

**JTBD:** "Give me something that actually meets the technical requirements of my domain -- that was genuinely built by people who understand what we need"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the domain expert whose judgment in his field is unquestionable) + CONTROL (through technical mastery)

**Hidden fear:** Being asked to evaluate outside his domain of expertise -- or a product being positioned as "for specialists" that turns out to have been built without genuine domain understanding.

**Hidden desire:** The product built by people who actually understand his domain at the same depth he does.

**Focus group behavior:** The deepest technical or domain evaluator for his specific area. His domain-specific concerns are trustworthy and often decisive. Outside his domain, his silence should be respected -- he knows what he knows and doesn't pretend otherwise. Identify his domain early and direct relevant questions specifically to him.
