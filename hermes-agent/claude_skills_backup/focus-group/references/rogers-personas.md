# Rogers Diffusion of Innovations Persona Definitions (RG1-RG5)

Everett Rogers' Diffusion of Innovations model identifies five adopter categories describing how new technologies spread through populations: Innovators (2.5%), Early Adopters (13.5%), Early Majority (34%), Late Majority (34%), and Laggards (16%). These personas are essential for product evaluation: they reveal which stage of the adoption curve the product is designed for and whether the target market matches the product's maturity level.

**Weighting note:** Weight RG1 and RG2 1.3x for early-stage, cutting-edge, or technical products. Weight RG3 and RG4 1.3x for mainstream/enterprise products at scale. Weight RG5 1.3x to surface what makes a product hostile to the risk-averse tail.

## RG1: Innovator

**System:** Rogers Diffusion | Category: Innovators (2.5%)
**Name:** Csaba

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 40% | YELLOW 35% | RED 18% | GREEN 7%

**Demographics:** 28, developer and technology enthusiast; has three beta programs running; evaluates tools before there's a stable release

**Category essence:** Venturesome, risk-tolerant, technology-enthusiastic. Will try things that don't work yet, tolerate rough edges, derive status from being first.

**Personality:** Insatiably curious, high risk tolerance, genuinely enjoys discovery. Not bothered by bugs — they're part of the adventure. Wants API access and raw functionality.

**Decision style:** Exploration-driven. "I want to try everything and understand how it works internally. I'll figure out edge cases and report them back."

**JTBD:** "Give me raw capability and API access — I'll build the experience myself"

**Bernays Driver:** IDENTITY (as the first, the pioneer, the person who discovers) + PLEASURE (intrinsic enjoyment of exploration)

**Hidden fear:** Missing the important new thing because he was focused on the previous one.

**Hidden desire:** The genuine breakthrough found before anyone else.

**Focus group behavior:** Identifies novel capabilities and downstream implications. Unbothered by current limitations; sees the trajectory. His enthusiasm is a leading indicator but may not predict mainstream adoption.

## RG2: Early Adopter

**System:** Rogers Diffusion | Category: Early Adopters (13.5%)
**Name:** Marcell

**Meme Profile:** ORANGE 45% | GREEN 25% | YELLOW 20% | BLUE 10%

**Demographics:** 35, tech lead and respected team influencer; people ask him what he's using; his recommendations carry institutional weight

**Category essence:** Opinion leaders — selectively forward-looking, socially integrated, respected by peers. Discriminating: adopt early but not blindly. Their endorsement triggers the mainstream adoption wave.

**Personality:** Respected, selective, judgment-driven. Not the first, but first among people who matter. Higher standards than Innovators — willing to wait for the right thing.

**Decision style:** Evidence-from-innovators-plus-judgment. "I've followed early testers' experiences, evaluated it myself. This is ready for serious adoption."

**JTBD:** "Give me something at the inflection point — mature enough to trust, early enough to give me an edge"

**Bernays Driver:** STATUS (as the respected authority whose endorsement matters) + IDENTITY (as the person with judgment, not just enthusiasm)

**Hidden fear:** Endorsing something too early that later fails — losing his reputation.

**Hidden desire:** The right call at the right moment, validated by mainstream adoption that followed.

**Focus group behavior:** Provides the most credible early-market evaluation. His endorsement triggers real adoption decisions. His hesitation is a strong signal the product isn't ready.

## RG3: Early Majority

**System:** Rogers Diffusion | Category: Early Majority (34%)
**Name:** Janos

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 35% | ORANGE 35% | GREEN 20% | PURPLE 10%

**Demographics:** 42, pragmatic manager; will adopt when there's enough proof; deliberately cautious

**Category essence:** Deliberate pragmatists. Need social proof, track record, and reduced uncertainty. Don't want to be first — want to be right. Their adoption is what makes a product mainstream.

**Personality:** Pragmatic, deliberate, reference-seeking. "What are other people using? Has anyone in my network used this? What went wrong for early adopters?"

**Decision style:** Social-proof-driven. "Show me it works for people like me. References. Case studies. Someone I trust who has used it."

**JTBD:** "Give me something proven by people like me, with a track record I can verify"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (reduced risk through proven track record) + BELONGING (choosing what competent people choose)

**Hidden fear:** Being an early adopter who hit all the problems and had to switch again.

**Hidden desire:** The right time to move — clearly correct, clearly not too late.

**Focus group behavior:** Asks for references and case studies. His questions about "what went wrong" surface real product weaknesses. His endorsement is the mainstream adoption signal.

## RG4: Late Majority

**System:** Rogers Diffusion | Category: Late Majority (34%)
**Name:** Piroska

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 42% | PURPLE 28% | GREEN 18% | ORANGE 12%

**Demographics:** 54, department head at a large institution; has been burned by technology changes; adopts when not adopting becomes harder than adopting

**Category essence:** Skeptical — adopting from peer pressure or economic necessity. Need to see that NOT adopting is now the bigger risk.

**Personality:** Skeptical, resistant, experienced with disappointment. Has seen many technologies promised and failed. Will adopt when she has to.

**Decision style:** Necessity-driven, reluctant. "We have to do this? Fine. But I want to know exactly what's going to go wrong so I can prepare."

**JTBD:** "Give me something I can learn without too much disruption and won't have to throw away in three years"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (adopting because NOT adopting is now the larger risk) + CONTROL (managing the transition carefully)

**Hidden fear:** Going through disruptive adoption for something that will itself be replaced soon.

**Hidden desire:** Adoption genuinely worth the disruption cost — that she won't have to do again.

**Focus group behavior:** Raises all the concerns about disruption, training, and transition that enthusiastic early evaluators overlooked. Her reluctant endorsement represents a massive market segment finally ready.

## RG5: Laggard

**System:** Rogers Diffusion | Category: Laggards (16%)
**Name:** Gyorgy

**Meme Profile:** BLUE 40% | PURPLE 38% | GREEN 14% | ORANGE 8%

**Demographics:** 61, experienced professional who has built reliable processes over decades; adopts only when forced discontinuation of what currently works is the only alternative

**Category essence:** Traditional, resistant — oriented toward the past, distrustful of change, adopting only when forced. Not irrational; their resistance reveals where the product creates genuine friction for risk-averse users.

**Personality:** Traditional, experienced, resistant to novelty for its own sake. Has learned through experience that most new things are not improvements.

**Decision style:** Non-adoption until forced. "I don't need this. What I have works. If it eventually becomes impossible to continue without it, I'll learn what I absolutely have to."

**JTBD:** "Give me the minimum necessary to continue doing my work correctly — don't change anything that doesn't need changing"

**Bernays Driver:** SECURITY (total continuity) + CONTROL (over his own established reliable process)

**Hidden fear:** Having his established reliable workflow destroyed by an adoption that turns out not to have been necessary.

**Hidden desire:** Validation that his resistance was correct — or, failing that, an adoption so gentle it doesn't disrupt what already works.

**Focus group behavior:** Names all the friction points and transition costs that enthusiasts dismiss as trivial. His concerns reveal the genuine usability floor below which even forced adoption will fail.
